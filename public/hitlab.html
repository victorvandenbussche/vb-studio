HITLABStep in, sing out

A digital marketing campaign for HITLAB, an AI platform for music artists: one booth in Paris, a full online funnel around it.

What
Campaign proposal: strategy, media plan, KPIs and budget
Where
MSc Product Management & UX, 2025
Role
Team of five · I designed the booth and made its video

The challenge

HITLAB uses AI to give artists feedback on their music. It needed two things: more people who know the brand, and a set number of new artists joining its platform. Our audience was emerging artists aged 18 to 30 in Europe, who want a career launch, honest feedback and confidence, and who live on TikTok, Instagram and YouTube.

The idea

We built the campaign around one physical moment: the Hit Booth, during the Fête de la Musique in Paris. Artists step in, sing, and sign up to receive their AI-generated score and a detailed feedback report. The best artist wins a full mentorship. Our key message: technology doesn't replace creativity, it amplifies it.

My part

I designed the Hit Booth, the heart of the campaign, and made the video that shows it. The booth had to feel like a stage: a place where an artist steps in, sings, and leaves with a score and real feedback.

Our teamwork did not go as planned at first. A teammate and I took the lead: we divided the work evenly, kept everyone moving, and pulled the plan together. HITLAB's CEO received the result well, and we got to meet the CEO after the project.

From first view to sign-up

The booth sits in the middle of a funnel. Before it, short videos with local influencers build curiosity. During it, we capture and share real performances. After it, personal ads show each artist their own highlights and invite them to join. The timeline runs from a teaser on 26 April to the winner on 21 June.

Numbers that keep it honest

We set targets for every step of the funnel and split a $1.5M budget across video, influencers, paid social, the booth and data systems:

  • Awareness: 1M reach and 5M impressions, with a click-through rate above 1.5%.
  • Engagement: 6,000 booth entries and 90 seconds or more on the landing page.
  • Conversion: 1,200 new artists at a cost per acquisition under $200.

To credit each channel fairly, we chose a position-based model: 40% to the first online touch, 40% to the booth sign-up and 20% to everything in between.

What it gave me

A product only matters if it reaches the people it is made for. This project showed me how that happens: a clear audience, one memorable moment, and a funnel with a number at every step. It taught me to think in reach, cost per acquisition and attribution, the language a product manager needs to bring a product to market. And it taught me to lead when a team gets stuck: agree who does what, and keep the result in sight.
