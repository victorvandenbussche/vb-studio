MyRampDesigning for independence

A user-centred product design project that taught me the importance of empathy, structure and the value of bringing products to life.

What
Mobile, lightweight ramp for active wheelchair users
Where
Internship at My Add On
Role
Individual project, Bachelor in Industrial Product Design

The project

MyRamp is a mobile, compact and lightweight ramp designed for active wheelchair users. Developed during my internship at My Add On as part of my bachelor's degree in Industrial Product Design, the project focused on addressing a simple but meaningful challenge: helping wheelchair users overcome everyday obstacles and maintain their independence.

Throughout this project, I explored the product from different perspectives, from initial concepts and sketches to prototypes, user testing and design iterations. I worked on various aspects of the experience, including the ramp's ergonomics, folding mechanism, handles, storage system and user guidance. Every design decision was an opportunity to make the product more practical, intuitive and accessible.

What it taught me

But MyRamp was about more than designing a product. It was also an important step in discovering what kind of professional I wanted to become.

At the beginning, my enthusiasm often pushed me straight into generating ideas and building prototypes. I quickly realised that having many ideas does not necessarily lead to the best solutions. Through user feedback and the application of the INNOWIZ design methodology, I learned to approach challenges more systematically, question my assumptions and make more thoughtful design decisions.

One of my biggest takeaways was understanding that good design is not about finding the fastest solution, but about finding the right solution for the people who will use it. Testing, listening, questioning and iterating became essential parts of my process.

However, the experience also made me reflect on something beyond the design itself. I realised that I was missing an important part of the bigger picture: understanding how to bring a product into the market and ensure that its value translates into something meaningful for its users. I became increasingly interested in what happens beyond the design process: understanding user needs, shaping the product vision, creating a compelling user story and connecting a product's potential to real-world value.

I went from wanting to design great products to wanting to understand how great products create value, reach people and become meaningful parts of their lives.

Why Product Management

This realisation played an important role in my decision to pursue a career in Product Management.

I discovered that I wanted to do more than design products. I wanted to help shape what gets built, why it gets built and how it creates value. I wanted to connect user needs, business objectives and product development to turn ideas into meaningful experiences.

MyRamp reflects an important chapter in my development: the moment I began looking beyond the product itself and became interested in the bigger picture of creating, positioning and delivering value.

Today, I bring a designer's mindset into my approach to Product Management: curiosity, creativity, empathy, a hands-on mentality and a strong desire to understand people. I believe great products are not simply designed; they are shaped around a clear purpose, a genuine user need and a meaningful story.
