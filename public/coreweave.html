CoreWeaveCan an AI cloud win?

A strategy analysis of CoreWeave, the fast-growing cloud built for AI. Can it keep growing, and what protects it from much bigger rivals?

What
Strategy analysis of a listed AI cloud company
Where
MSc Product Management & UX, 2025
Role
Team of five · I led the industry analysis

The question

CoreWeave rents out huge clusters of GPUs to AI companies like Microsoft and Meta. In 2025 it had more than 250,000 GPUs and a backlog of over $55 billion, but it was not yet profitable and depended on a few very large clients. Our team had to judge its financial health and strategic position against AWS, Microsoft, DigitalOcean and Nebius.

My part: the industry analysis

I owned the section on the market CoreWeave plays in. I worked from SEC filings and market data and turned them into three answers:

  • Performance: CoreWeave's share outpaced the S&P 500 throughout the year, carried by demand for AI infrastructure.
  • Growth: revenue grew about 235% a year from 2020 to 2024, and forecasts expect about 38% a year up to $5B in 2027.
  • Pressure: a Porter's five forces analysis, with one radar chart per force, showed where the power sits.

The finding that stood out: NVIDIA holds the most power. Supplier power came out high, while buyers, substitutes and rivalry scored medium to high, and the threat of new entrants low to medium. Whoever gets the GPUs wins, which made supply the key risk in our recommendations.

What the team recommended

Together we advised CoreWeave to position itself as the AI specialist cloud, win more enterprise customers, lower its operating costs through energy and more GPU suppliers, and grow internationally.

What it gave me

As a designer I was used to asking what we should build. This project made me ask whether a business can win, and why. I learned to read a company through its filings and cash flow, and to turn a framework like Porter's five forces into one clear judgement that a team can build on.
